What the strategy does

FABLE Core is a long-only, unleveraged portfolio of Indian shares and gold that follows three rules.

  • Own recent winners. From the 250 most liquid NSE stocks, hold 15 large-cap and 15 mid-cap names with the strongest 6- and 12-month momentum, adjusted for volatility. Review monthly; a holding is kept until it falls well down the ranking.
  • Step aside when the market weakens. Each day, check the Nifty 500 against five moving averages (100 to 300 days). Equity exposure equals the share of them it is above, so the portfolio scales out in 20% steps as a decline deepens and back in as it recovers.
  • Always hold 25% gold. Gold has tended to hold up when Indian equities fall, and costs nothing in long-run return in this test.
Whatever the trend rule removes sits in cash. Typical activity: about 30 holdings, average holding period about two months, no shorting, no derivatives, no intraday trading.

About 95 strategy configurations across ten families were tested. Three ideas survived and are combined in the portfolio; the rest were rejected, mostly on costs or fragility.

How it was tested

The aim was to find reasons not to trust each result. These are the controls that were applied.

Against look-ahead
  • Signals use data up to a day's close; trades execute at the next day's open.
  • An automated test truncates the future and confirms no past signal changes.
  • The machine-learning test was walk-forward with a 45-day embargo between training and test data.
Against survivorship bias
  • A basket of today's Nifty 500 members "earns" 24.4% a year in hindsight; the real equal-weight index earned 14.6%.
  • Our stock-level momentum backtest overstated the official index by about 10% a year (2020–26: 27.1% vs 16.2%), while matching its pattern (monthly correlation 0.92).
  • So every expected-return figure on this page comes from NSE's official indices, which have no such bias.
Realistic costs
  • Securities transaction tax, stamp duty, exchange and regulator fees, GST, and depository charges on every sale.
  • Slippage of 5 to 15 basis points per side by liquidity; orders capped at a small share of daily volume.
  • Total cost drag of the strategy: about 1.3% a year at 239% turnover.
Against data-mining
  • With 95 configurations tried, luck alone would produce a Sharpe ratio near 0.54. The chosen portfolio's is 0.91; the index's is 0.46.
  • Parameters were not optimised: the trend rule averages five lengths instead of picking the best one.
  • Failed strategies are reported, not hidden.

Engineering checks
  • The backtester and the paper-trading order engine are separate programs; over the replay year they agree to within 0.2% (daily correlation 0.998). That comparison caught and fixed a stock-split bug.
  • 18 automated tests cover timing, costs, risk limits, duplicate-order prevention and book reconciliation.
  • All figures regenerate from one script.

Risk controls

A separate risk module must approve every order; the strategy cannot bypass it.
  • No leverage; no single stock above 10%; no sector above 35%; minimum liquidity for every stock.
  • New buying stops after a 4% daily or 7% weekly loss, or an 8% index move.
  • Equity is halved at a 15% portfolio drawdown and sold entirely at 25%, after which a person must restart it.
  • Trading is blocked if market data is stale, and an emergency stop blocks all orders.

Limitations

  • This is paper trading. No real money is invested. Simulated fills can be better than real ones.
  • Momentum's edge has shrunk since these indices became investable, and may shrink further. 2013–2019 returned 10.6% a year, below the index.
  • The drawdown protection rests on two bear markets (2008 and 2011). A crash faster than the trend rule can react would not be avoided; the worst single day was -9.7%.
  • Tax is not included above. If all gains are taxed as short-term at 20%, 16.6% a year becomes about 13.4%, close to what a buy-and-hold index investor keeps (12.8%).
  • Not tested: pairs trading, earnings and news strategies, and anything needing intraday or order-book data.
